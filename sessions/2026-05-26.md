## Themes

**1. The incident.**
Yinnie went to sell a TV (approximately five pounds) in a public car park near her home. A man she did not know approached confidently; she mistook him for the buyer. He was not. He asked intrusive personal questions (where she was from, guessed China, pointed out a birthmark on her face). She could not disengage cleanly because the confusion persisted. The actual buyer appeared and the transaction completed, but she left shaken. She walked home checking behind her. Called her best friend, who stayed on the phone. Cyrus was at training and unreachable.

**2. The fear is about the future, not the present.**
She said it precisely: "My anxiety is not about not feeling safe at home. My anxiety is the fact that I need to leave my house." The car park is opposite her regular supermarket. She fears the man lives locally and she will encounter him again. The birthmark makes her feel identifiable. The loss she named is baseline safety in her own neighbourhood.

**3. Knowledge-feeling gap in real time.**
Asked the coach directly: "Do you think I am catastrophising?" Knew intellectually the threat level was low. Could not feel it. Purest form of the session 1 pattern - accurate self-diagnosis with zero protective function.

**4. Self-blame activated immediately.**
"I feel really stupid for doing something for about five pounds." "I feel stupid that I even entertained the conversation." Dispositional attribution in a context where she genuinely did nothing wrong. She chose a public place, daylight, had the buyer's car details. The confusion about the man's identity was reasonable.

**5. Pre-calculation of Cyrus's response.**
Before Cyrus responded, she predicted: "He's just gonna tell me everything's gonna be okay and that he'll always protect me, but he can't." Same pre-calculation structure as sessions 8 and 10 but in acute fear context. Could not answer when asked what she actually needed from him.

**6. Accommodation under threat.**
Even during harassment, she accommodated - answered questions, played the guessing game, offered the TV twice. Said the only reason was confusion about his identity, but the difficulty disengaging and automatic politeness under pressure is the accommodation reflex operating in a safety-relevant context.

## Emotional Register

Arrived scared and activated - call made within minutes of getting home. Speech was rapid, looping, detail-heavy - adrenaline-driven narration, not the scattered depletion of sessions 10-11. Corrected the coach firmly when he misunderstood the situation ("you're not understanding me" and "this is not the guy buying my stuff"). This precision under distress is notable. By end of session, adrenaline settling but catastrophising continuing. Asked for reality-testing, accepted the answer, chose to get lost in work. No flat compliance. No "sure" or "yep" without conviction. Very different presentation from the last three sessions.
