# Pattern: Compounding Anxiety Loop

**First observed:** 2026-03-27 (session 1)
**Acute reactivation risk:** 2026-05-26 (session 12)

## Description

Yinnie explicitly described a mechanism in which anxiety from one episode is not discharged but carried forward and amplified into the next similar situation. She used the phrase "the replay then feeds into the next scenario" and "it will just amplify that and continue that negative loop."

## Examples from Sessions

- **Session 1:** Meeting self-doubt - did not speak, replayed the meeting, described the replay as raising the anxiety baseline for the next meeting.
- **Session 2:** The loop shifted from episodic to baseline. No longer specific replay events - a persistent low state: "every morning" when she wakes up. Two and a half years of compounding may have raised the floor to a chronic state.
- **Session 10:** Most concerning disclosure. "Dead on the inside, which is not untrue." A colleague noticed the change unprompted. Could not identify a single thing that would make the week lighter.
- **Session 11:** "Nothing has changed" from session 10. Cognitive impairment named for the first time - "I'm really struggling to think clearly." Also spent significant session time pre-calculating the Joe meeting - rehearsing failure scenarios, raising anxiety baseline before the actual event.
- **Session 12 (acute reactivation risk):** Harassed by a stranger in a car park near her home. The incident is located in her daily routine space (opposite her regular supermarket). She explicitly named the compounding fear: "I'm never gonna go back to feeling like that again" and "I will be more anxious than I am before." She also said: "My anxiety is not about not feeling safe at home. My anxiety is the fact that I need to leave my house." This is the session 1 mechanism applied to physical safety: one scary incident threatens to rewrite her baseline for all future trips outside.

## Clinical Notes

The progression across sessions: episodic spirals (session 1) to chronic baseline (session 2) to "dead on the inside" (session 10) to cognitive impairment (session 11). Session 12 introduces a new acute trigger on top of the chronic depression.

The risk with the session 12 incident is neighbourhood avoidance. If she begins avoiding the car park / supermarket area, the avoidance will reinforce the anxiety and compound the baseline further. She already named this as her fear. The proximity to her home makes this high-stakes - this is not an avoidable location.

The net negative framing interacts with this loop: each setback is not just anxiety-provoking but confirmatory of a feared identity outcome, which prevents discharge.

The accommodation-resentment pattern (cross-domain) is a maintaining factor: she carries unacknowledged relational costs across multiple domains, and the cumulative weight contributes to the mood floor.

Therapy referral obtained as of session 11 but no appointment booked yet. Session 12 was a crisis call and therapy was not discussed. Four sessions now at severe mood or crisis with no treatment. If treatment does not start soon, the coach may need to raise alternative access routes despite her resistance to being pushed on this.

## Watch For

- Baseline mood check each session: stable, rising, or falling?
- Neighbourhood avoidance: has she been to the supermarket area since the incident?
- Whether the incident is processed as discrete or compounds into the depressive baseline.
- Cognitive impairment: is "struggling to think clearly" persistent?
- Whether therapy actually starts.
- Pre-calculation of failure as an active variant: rehearsing worst-case scenarios that raise the baseline before the actual event.
- Whether specific episodic spirals are still occurring or whether the presentation is fully chronic flatness.
- New avoidance behaviours triggered by the incident.
