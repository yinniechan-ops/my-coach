# Pattern: Compounding Anxiety Loop

**First observed:** 2026-03-27 (session 1)

## Description

Yinnie explicitly described a mechanism in which anxiety from one episode is not discharged but carried forward and amplified into the next similar situation. She used the phrase "the replay then feeds into the next scenario" and "it will just amplify that and continue that negative loop."

## Examples from Sessions

- **Session 1:** Meeting self-doubt - did not speak, replayed the meeting, described the replay as raising the anxiety baseline for the next meeting.
- **Session 2:** The loop shifted from episodic to baseline. No longer specific replay events - a persistent low state: "every morning" when she wakes up. Two and a half years of compounding may have raised the floor to a chronic state.
- **Session 10:** Most concerning disclosure. "Dead on the inside, which is not untrue." A colleague noticed the change unprompted. Could not identify a single thing that would make the week lighter.
- **Session 11:** "Nothing has changed" from session 10. NEW: cognitive impairment named for the first time - "I'm really struggling to think clearly." The depression is now affecting function, not just mood. Also spent significant session time pre-calculating the Joe meeting - rehearsing failure scenarios, raising anxiety baseline before the actual event. This is the session 1 mechanism in a new form.

## Clinical Notes

The progression across sessions: episodic spirals (session 1) to chronic baseline (session 2) to "dead on the inside" (session 10) to cognitive impairment (session 11). The floor may still be falling.

The net negative framing interacts with this loop: each setback is not just anxiety-provoking but confirmatory of a feared identity outcome, which prevents discharge.

The accommodation-resentment pattern (cross-domain) is a maintaining factor: she carries unacknowledged relational costs across multiple domains, and the cumulative weight contributes to the mood floor.

Therapy referral obtained as of session 11 but no appointment booked yet. Three sessions at severe mood with no treatment. If treatment does not start soon, the coach may need to raise alternative access routes despite her resistance to being pushed on this.

## Watch For

- Baseline mood check each session: stable, rising, or falling?
- Cognitive impairment: is "struggling to think clearly" persistent or was it illness-related (she had a cold)?
- Whether therapy actually starts.
- Pre-calculation of failure as an active variant: rehearsing worst-case scenarios that raise the baseline before the actual event.
- Whether specific episodic spirals are still occurring or whether the presentation is fully chronic flatness.
- The "dead on the inside" disclosure warrants close monitoring. If the next session shows similar or worse, the coach should ask directly about the therapy timeline.
