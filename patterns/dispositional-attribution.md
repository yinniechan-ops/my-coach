# Pattern: Dispositional Attribution

**First observed:** 2026-03-27 (session 1)
**Origin event identified:** 2026-04-29 (session 8a)
**Full activation observed:** 2026-05-18 (session 11)

## Description

When Yinnie hits a real obstacle or feels blocked, she routes the explanation through personal failure - character, willingness, or identity - rather than situational factors or specific skill gaps. The attribution is internal and global, not external or specific.

Domain-specific: most active in professional and entrepreneurial contexts where external confirmation is absent. Quieter in interpersonal contexts where the other person's behaviour is clearly the relevant factor.

## Origin Event

Session 8 revealed a likely origin: her ex-cofounder was demonstrably not invested, deleted their waitlist without remorse, and admitted he didn't care. Rather than processing this as his failure of commitment, the experience has become a generalised expectation: "I don't think people want to work with me because I honestly don't bring anything other than my pure grit." Someone else's failure of investment has been internalised as evidence of her own inadequacy.

## Examples from Sessions

- **Session 1:** Cannot get users - concludes she does not want it badly enough. Will not post on LinkedIn - frames as character flaw.
- **Session 2:** Low sign-up rate becomes "maybe it's just not the right thing" and "should I give up."
- **Session 3:** Cofounder ambiguity routes to "maybe it's not me."
- **Session 7:** Rachel call: "I didn't sell myself," "I fumbled it."
- **Session 8:** "I'm playing all my cards wrong." "I don't bring anything other than pure grit." Deepest activations.
- **Session 11 (full activation):** Every ambiguous workplace signal routed through personal failure. Joe booking a meeting = she failed. Rob escalating = she didn't manage well enough. Stepping back from Rob = bad PM work. Product committee challenge = she didn't fight hard enough. "I'll always be that PM that is not technical" - a permanent identity-level deficit.

## The "Not Technical" Variant

Resurfaced in session 11 after first appearing in session 1. "I'll always be that PM that is not technical. I hate having to say this all the time." This functions as a fixed, permanent deficit rather than a situational gap. It sits underneath PM failure feelings as the deepest layer. The coach challenged with specific evidence (she diagnosed three data sources, raised consistency argument in committee, read Rob's personality correctly) but emotional movement was absent.

This variant intersects with net negative framing: like "I don't bring anything other than pure grit," "not technical" is experienced as a permanent inadequacy that cannot be closed.

## Quieter Instances

- **Session 4:** Attributed Cyrus's behaviour to Cyrus, appropriately.
- **Session 5:** Handled cofounder closure without self-blame.

## Clinical Notes

The pattern has deepened across sessions. Session 11 was the most activated instance: not one attribution but a cascade, with every element of a complex workplace situation routing through "I'm failing." The coach challenged with evidence on each point and got partial cognitive traction but no emotional movement - the knowledge-feeling gap compounds with dispositional attribution to make it resistant to in-session reframing.

The domain-specificity remains: most active when external confirmation is absent, quieter when the other person's behaviour is clearly the variable.

The "not technical" belief may need specific behavioural intervention: what specific technical knowledge would have changed the outcome? The answer is usually nothing - which, if demonstrated repeatedly, might erode the belief more than reassurance.

## Watch For

- "I don't bring anything" or variants.
- "Not technical" as the substrate under PM failure.
- Jumping from specific obstacle to global self-verdict.
- Cascade attributions: multiple ambiguous signals all routing through failure simultaneously.
- Whether specific evidence challenge gets any emotional traction over time, not just cognitive.
- "Playing my cards wrong" - blends dispositional and strategic.
