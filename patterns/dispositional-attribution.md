# Pattern: Dispositional Attribution

**First observed:** 2026-03-27 (session 1)
**Origin event identified:** 2026-04-29 (session 8a)
**Full activation observed:** 2026-05-18 (session 11)
**Acute context observed:** 2026-05-26 (session 12)

## Description

When Yinnie hits a real obstacle or feels blocked, she routes the explanation through personal failure - character, willingness, or identity - rather than situational factors or specific skill gaps. The attribution is internal and global, not external or specific.

Domain-specific: most active in professional and entrepreneurial contexts where external confirmation is absent. Quieter in interpersonal contexts where the other person's behaviour is clearly the relevant factor. Session 12 shows it activates in acute safety contexts too - she blamed herself for being harassed.

## Origin Event

Session 8 revealed a likely origin: her ex-cofounder was demonstrably not invested, deleted their waitlist without remorse, and admitted he didn't care. Rather than processing this as his failure of commitment, the experience has become a generalised expectation: "I don't think people want to work with me because I honestly don't bring anything other than my pure grit." Someone else's failure of investment has been internalised as evidence of her own inadequacy.

## Examples from Sessions

- **Session 1:** Cannot get users - concludes she does not want it badly enough. Will not post on LinkedIn - frames as character flaw.
- **Session 2:** Low sign-up rate becomes "maybe it's just not the right thing" and "should I give up."
- **Session 3:** Cofounder ambiguity routes to "maybe it's not me."
- **Session 7:** Rachel call: "I didn't sell myself," "I fumbled it."
- **Session 8:** "I'm playing all my cards wrong." "I don't bring anything other than pure grit." Deepest activations.
- **Session 11 (full activation):** Every ambiguous workplace signal routed through personal failure. Joe booking a meeting = she failed. Rob escalating = she didn't manage well enough. "I'll always be that PM that is not technical" - permanent identity-level deficit.
- **Session 12 (acute context):** Immediately blamed herself for being harassed: "I feel really stupid for doing something for about five pounds." "I feel stupid that I even entertained the conversation." She did everything right - public place, daylight, buyer details, friend on phone. The self-blame was automatic and instantaneous, faster than in workplace contexts.

## The "Not Technical" Variant

Resurfaced in session 11 after first appearing in session 1. "I'll always be that PM that is not technical. I hate having to say this all the time." Functions as a fixed, permanent deficit rather than a situational gap. Sits underneath PM failure feelings as the deepest layer.

## Quieter Instances

- **Session 4:** Attributed Cyrus's behaviour to Cyrus, appropriately.
- **Session 5:** Handled cofounder closure without self-blame.

## Clinical Notes

The pattern has deepened across sessions and now activates across all domains including acute safety. Session 12 is notable because the self-blame was the fastest observed - within minutes of the incident, before any processing. The automaticity suggests it is deeply ingrained.

The session 12 instance is also the clearest case of the attribution being factually wrong: she genuinely did nothing wrong and named herself as stupid. The evidence contradiction is unambiguous, which makes it a useful reference point for future challenge.

The domain-specificity is weakening: originally most active when external confirmation was absent, it now activates even in contexts where external factors are obvious (a stranger harassed her).

## Watch For

- "I don't bring anything" or variants.
- "Not technical" as the substrate under PM failure.
- Jumping from specific obstacle to global self-verdict.
- Cascade attributions: multiple ambiguous signals all routing through failure simultaneously.
- Self-blame in safety contexts - "I was stupid" when she was not.
- Whether specific evidence challenge gets any emotional traction over time.
- "Playing my cards wrong" - blends dispositional and strategic.
- Speed of activation: how quickly does self-blame arrive after an event?
